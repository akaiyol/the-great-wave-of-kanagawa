# Wave iteration retrospective

2026-10-26. Scope: the saved thirteen-candidate experiment ledger, retained candidate 12, raw/native captures, independent reviews, and current agent instructions. No new art implementation or subagent was launched for this audit.

## Assessment

The run produced useful technical safeguards and modest local art gains. It did not produce a proportionate improvement in overall resemblance to the reference. More agents or more iterations would not automatically correct this.

Exact token efficiency is unknown: this audit has no per-agent input/output/cache/reasoning-token trace. Shared account allowance percentages cannot establish this project's consumption or convert to token counts. Instruction word reduction is not measured token savings. The judgments below concern observed work and outcomes, not financial ROI.

The native baseline and retained sample still share much of the same silhouette. Candidate 02 fixed connectivity, 08/09 improved foreground taper while protecting middle depth, 10 adjusted cove heads, and 11/12 introduced a more useful branch-authoring representation. Candidates 03, 06 and 07 mostly altered symptoms. Candidate 04 revealed the need to check black-water openness separately from white-foam connectivity. Candidates 05 and 13 remain visually unreviewed; they are not successful art outcomes.

## Did we use too many subagents?

Concurrency was bounded, and reusing one worker with fresh independent reviewers was sensible. The problem was delegation frequency relative to information gained. Several full comparisons rediscovered the same smooth-cap/pendant failure without changing the next hypothesis enough. Small edits also involved lead specification, worker implementation, lead inspection and reviewer inspection, creating repeated handoffs around largely serial work.

Keep independent review for promotion, substantive representation changes, and ambiguous regressions. Let the lead reject obviously weak candidates after cheap triage. Use one worker for a bounded geometry task; add a researcher only for a distinct unresolved question. Two children are a ceiling, not a target. Do not delegate counting, hashes, fixture enumeration or routine receipt checks to an LLM.

## Where effort was poorly allocated

1. **The visual objective was under-specified.** Technical topology was measurable; reference fidelity was described broadly. Reviewers could say “marginally better” repeatedly without identifying a completed visual milestone.
2. **We stayed with the same failure too long.** Different control-point edits preserved the blank cap and hanging groups. The representation diagnosis came late.
3. **Positive calibration was mismatched.** The accepted 360 × 280 interior fragment neither supplied a positive full crest nor established readable detail inside smaller waves. The scale study was useful, but should have preceded more fine-detail edits.
4. **Review preparation was too repetitive.** Hash verification and coverage checks should arrive as compact scripted results. The reviewer should inspect a stable evidence package, with changed and worst-case panels identified without supplying a preferred verdict.
5. **The lead accumulated too much history.** Repeated file reads, large tool outputs, screenshot review and orchestration are plausible major model costs, but their ranking is unmeasured. Fourteen saved focused receipts report browser generation times of 225–537 ms, median 314.5 ms. That does not include model work or export time and is not a token-cost measurement.

## Engineering guidance and its application

- **Anthropic Engineering, [How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system), June 13, 2025:** independent breadth-first research benefits from delegation; highly dependent coding is a weaker fit. Their reported token multipliers concern their own workloads, not ours. Application: avoid a research-style fan-out for serial contour editing.
- **OpenAI, [Subagents](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/subagents):** isolated contexts can keep noisy exploration out of the lead conversation; concise returned summaries and caution around concurrent writes matter. Application: retain fresh bounded reviews and one writer, but keep the lead's working context small too.
- **Anthropic Engineering, [Effective context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents), September 29, 2025:** retrieve relevant information as needed and preserve compact external state. Application: load current candidate, current failure, last decision and next experiment; retrieve historical evidence by path rather than repeatedly reading the ledger and receipts in full.
- **Anthropic Engineering, [Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), January 9, 2026:** outcomes, transcripts and graders answer different questions; subjective model grading needs calibration against expert judgment. Application: technical pass, visible art improvement and completed ocean remain separate outcomes. A repeated preference is not evidence of absolute fidelity.
- **Vivek Trivedy, LangChain, [Improving Deep Agents with harness engineering](https://www.langchain.com/blog/improving-deep-agents-with-harness-engineering), February 17, 2026:** analyze traces to locate failure modes, make targeted changes and verify against the requested behavior; avoid overfitting. Application: classify failures before editing and reserve held-out scenery for milestone checks. Their benchmark gains do not predict our visual gains.
- **Taylor Mullen and Christian Gunderman, Google, [The Anatomy of Harness Engineering](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/the-anatomy-of-harness-engineering-how-to-evaluate-iterate-and-guard-ai-coding-agents/), September 9, 2026:** narrow behavioral checks complement end-to-end evaluation and help explain regressions. Application: keep connectivity/openness tests as guardrails while judging actual source correspondence and ocean composition separately.

## Next iteration

1. **Freeze the working renderer and evaluation contract.** Keep source, fixtures and evidence versioned. Change the harness only when an observed gap can affect a decision.
2. **Establish one source-aligned positive branch group at target scale.** Annotate its shared root, unequal primaries, secondary attachment, recurved tip and black opening on the unchanged scan. Review authored boundaries over the source. Sample the same structure at intended display sizes. Do not infer exterior foam identity from a global brightness threshold.
3. **Declare a visible success condition before editing.** For the next group: distinct unequal primary branches and an attached secondary must remain recognizable at native foreground scale, with a curved black opening organizing them. All fixed topology checks remain required. A head nudge or added curl without that hierarchy is not the milestone.
4. **Compare representations before further tuning.** Compare retained 12 and archived 13 against that source-aligned control. After two edits repeat the same failure without the declared gain, stop that hypothesis. Diagnose source interpretation, representation or sampling instead of issuing another nearby control-point edit.
5. **Use review in stages.** Script model/topology/hash checks; let the builder or lead cheaply reject obvious failures; obtain an independent blinded review only for a candidate worth promoting. Preserve all fixed evidence and state the review's actual scope. Run full browser checks for promotions and renderer/composition changes.
6. **Then test generalization and the ocean.** Preserve the failing fixtures, add held-out seeds at milestones, and inspect consecutive full-viewport views. A locally attractive branch does not establish varied families, coherent depth or a vast ocean.

For each experiment, extend the existing ledger with the targeted failure, predicted visible change, actual decision, reviewer scope and stopping reason. Record model tokens and stage timings only when reliably available; otherwise mark them unknown. Periodically report meaningful art milestones and repeated-failure counts. Do not treat quota consumption, tests passed or candidate count as artistic progress.

No exponential improvement is established or promised. The highest-leverage opportunity is to make each experiment discriminate between competing explanations, so failed attempts change the next decision rather than repeat it.
